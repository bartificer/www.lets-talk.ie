# Wing&#8217;s Irish Drone Certification/Licensing Journey

<p>Ireland comes under <a href="https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones">EU rules for drone flying</a>, so Wing&#8217;s journey will be similar to that for other Europeans, though not identical, countries do get to add their own flavour!</p>

<p>Open A1/3 license → Open A2 → Specific Category</p>

<p>The Open A1/3 is easy to get, you just need to watch some videos, take a 30 minutes test, and pay a €30 registration fee. This lets you fly a small drone up to 120m high within visual range, during the day, in unrestricted airspace, and not anywhere near <em>uninvolved people</em>.</p>

<p>The Open A2 took more work — a more detailed course (still on a website, but paid for, Wing paid ~€100), and a longer test, but it allows flying within 5 meters of people. There is also another registration fee of €45.</p>

<p>This is all most hobbyists will need or want, but Wing went a step further (and maybe even one more beyond that soon!)</p>

<p>To get the <em>Specific Category</em> Wing needed to take a day-long course (~€600) with a certified trainer, and complete a <em>Specific Category Practical Skills Assessment</em> (basically a drone driving test ). Then you have to pay another registration fee to register as a drone operator which costs ~€300 every 2 years! This lets him fly after sunset and in restricted air space (with appropriate clearances!).</p>

<p>Wing&#8217;s also completed the needed paperwork to be certified to fly for leisure in the US (<a href="https://www.faa.gov/uas/recreational_flyers/knowledge_test_updates">TRUST</a>).</p>
