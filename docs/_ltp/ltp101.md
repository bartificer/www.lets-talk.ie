# Episode Notes

<p>Back in <a href="https://lets-talk.ie/ltp59">episode 59</a> I shared my thoughts on photographic projects in great detail. The definition for a <em>project</em> that I argued for was:</p>

<blockquote>
<p>A planned collection of related images shot over an extended period of time designed to be more than the sum of its parts.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>By that definition, starting a project isn&#8217;t something you do on a whim! But surely there&#8217;s something between shooting single images, and committing to a project? Yes! — shooting a series of images.</p>

<p>Series is a word, it has a meaning, but what do I mean by it? I&#8217;ve been thinking about that a lot in the last few months, because I&#8217;ve found myself thinking more and more in terms of small collections of photos that tell more of a story than the sum of their parts. In the end I settled on this simply definition:</p>

<blockquote>
<p>A series is a small collection of related images that make a logical whole, and can be easily shared as a unit, e.g. in a single tweet or IM to friends or family.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>Thanks to COVID my days have become very regimented in the past 2 years — I have just three <em>standard</em> morning walking routes, three similar lunchtime walking routes, and why my cycling routes are still dictated by the wind (always cycle into the wind on the out-bound leg!), Ireland&#8217;s winds are also very regimented, mostly south-westerlies with a few westerlies and the odd north-westerly to chill you to the bones!</p>

<p>How do you keep shooting interesting photos if you&#8217;re spending all your time in such a small range of places? Without thinking about what I was doing, I&#8217;ve found myself sharing more and more little series of photos on Twitter. Sometimes it&#8217;s two, three, or maybe four photos attached to a single Tweet, sometimes it&#8217;s a short thread with five or six photos, and when I get really inspired it&#8217;s a collage or a diptych/triptych.</p>

<p>Now that I&#8217;ve noticed the trend I&#8217;m starting to pro-actively think about little series I&#8217;d like to shoot, and ways to share them.</p>

<p>When it comes to sharing I&#8217;ve got nothing earth-shattering to add to the discussion:</p>

<ol>
<li>A few photos posted together somewhere</li>
<li>Two or three photos merged into a single image (diptych/triptych)</li>
<li>A Collage</li>
</ol>

<p>But I would like to suggest some ideas to get your creative juices flowing:</p>

<ol>
<li><strong>A collection of related things</strong> — <em>&#8220;all the X of Y&#8221;</em>, e.g.:

<ul>
<li>I did a big collage of all the different varieties of Daffodil growing in St. Patrick&#8217;s College Maynooth last spring (I found <strong>ten</strong>!): <a href="https://twitter.com/bbusschots/status/1376518675159773189?s=20&amp;t=BtslOqyIbn_Zmy89e_EDVg">twitter.com/…</a></li>
<li>Or this collection of all the different berries I found growing along the banks of the Royal Canal on a single walk last autumn: <a href="https://twitter.com/bbusschots/status/1431920894566338562?s=20&amp;t=BtslOqyIbn_Zmy89e_EDVg">twitter.com/…</a>.</li>
</ul></li>
<li><strong>Multiple points of view on the same thing</strong>, perhaps from different angles, or at different zoom levels. E.g.:

<ul>
<li>Three views I recently shot of the same former industrial mill along the Royal Canal in Dublin: <a href="https://twitter.com/bbusschots/status/1482334981615673344?s=20&amp;t=BtslOqyIbn_Zmy89e_EDVg">twitter.com/…</a></li>
<li>And three very different views of the same amazing morning sky over the boathouse and bridge in Carton House near Maynooth: <a href="https://twitter.com/bbusschots/status/1448910844696449032?s=20&amp;t=BtslOqyIbn_Zmy89e_EDVg">twitter.com/…</a></li>
</ul></li>
<li><strong>A time series</strong> — the same place or thing at different times of the day or year, maybe once in each season, or even just in different weather. Or the life-cycle of a single plant. E.g.:

<ul>
<li>The stages of a blackberry: <a href="https://twitter.com/bbusschots/status/1425443674486411271?s=20&amp;t=BtslOqyIbn_Zmy89e_EDVg">twitter.com/…</a>.</li>
<li>It could even be the simplest time series of all — a <em>before</em> and an <em>after</em> of some kind, like some delicious and beautiful purple cauliflower before and after being roasted: <a href="https://twitter.com/bbusschots/status/1428124134651871233?s=20&amp;t=BtslOqyIbn_Zmy89e_EDVg">twitter.com/…</a></li>
</ul></li>
<li><strong>A short story</strong> — <em>&#8220;three fun things I saw on my walk this morning&#8221;</em> etc.. E.g.:

<ul>
<li>The trains I saw on a recent walk: <a href="https://twitter.com/bbusschots/status/1492912583417991174?s=20&amp;t=BtslOqyIbn_Zmy89e_EDVg">twitter.com/…</a></li>
<li>Some of the buildings that caught my eye on a recent visit to the new TU Dublin campus in Grangegorman: <a href="https://twitter.com/bbusschots/status/1476161894935412739?s=20&amp;t=BtslOqyIbn_Zmy89e_EDVg">twitter.com/…</a></li>
</ul></li>
<li>An <strong>abstract theme</strong> like <em>&#8220;red things&#8221;</em> or <em>&#8220;things that made me smile today/this week&#8221;</em> (I&#8217;m very bad at abstraction, so I got no examples — maybe something I should work on in the coming months!)</li>
</ol>

<p>I hope this inspires you to think in terms of small series of images when you&#8217;re out and about shooting, and please @mention the show on Twitter when when share your work (<code>@ltpod</code>) &#x1f642;</p>
