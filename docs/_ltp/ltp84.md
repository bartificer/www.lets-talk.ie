<h4>The Poem</h4>

<p>The poem that inspired this instalment is <em>The Circus Animals&#8217; Desertion</em> by W. B. Yeats.</p>

<p>Below are the excerpts read on the podcast:</p>

<blockquote>
<p><strong>The Circus Animals&#8217; Desertion</strong>
<em>by William Buttler Yeats</em></p>

<p><strong>I</strong></p>

<p>I sought a theme and sought for it in vain,
I sought it daily for six weeks or so.
Maybe at last being but a broken man
I must be satisfied with my heart, although
Winter and summer till old age began
My circus animals were all on show,
Those stilted boys, that burnished chariot,
Lion and woman and the Lord knows what.</p>

<p><strong>II</strong></p>

<p>What can I but enumerate old themes,
…
<em>[a critical summary of his own career]</em>
…
Players and painted stage took all my love
And not those things that they were emblems of.</p>

<p><strong>III</strong></p>

<p>Those masterful images because complete
Grew in pure mind but out of what began?
A mound of refuse or the sweepings of a street,
Old kettles, old bottles, and a broken can,
Old iron, old bones, old rags, that raving slut
Who keeps the till. Now that my ladder&#8217;s gone
I must lie down where all the ladders start
In the foul rag and bone shop of the heart.</p>
</blockquote>

<p>You can <a href="https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poems/43299/the-circus-animals-desertion">read the full poem on the Poetry Foundation&#8217;s website</a>.</p>
