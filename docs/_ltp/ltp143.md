<h5>Allison&#39;s Initial Letter</h5>

<blockquote><p>Whilst walking and listening to the latest show, I thought of an idea for an episode: shooting in an urban environment. I’m not sure there’s a LOT of meat on them bones, but I thought about different things you can do in this realm.</p>

<p>We were in Fresno seeing [family]. I took this photo outside of our hotel.</p>

<p><img src="https://www.lets-talk.ie/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/LTP143-AllisonPhoto1.jpeg" alt="A pond under a blue sky with a line of trees on the far back, the trees are reflected in the pond but with a glassy look, and there are a few ducks breaking up the reflection near the far bank." /></p>

<p>It’s what my eye was drawn to and appreciated. But this is what the area actually looked like.</p>

<p><img src="https://www.lets-talk.ie/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/LTP143-AllisonPhoto2.jpeg" alt="The same scene as the first image, but zoomed right out to show a background with an industrial unit of some kind next to the trees on the far bank, a tattered chain-link fence in front of the water, and waste ground in the foreground." /></p>

<p>The point is that I often am focusing my joy of where I live on the beauty and my brain just throws away the ugly parts. Power poles just aren’t in my vision even though they may block the sunset. </p>

<p>The two photos I sent [previously] show how the different point of view can entirely change what one sees.</p>

<p><img src="https://www.lets-talk.ie/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/LTP143-AllisonPhoto3.jpeg" alt="A suburban intersection with a bed of flowers in the left foreground, a yellow pedestrian crossing in the right foreground, and a street with light grey cement pavements each side curving through from bottom left to top right. On the far side of the street are some nondescript back yards a red car, and some trees." /></p>

<p><img src="https://www.lets-talk.ie/blog/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/LTP143-AllisonPhoto4.jpeg" alt="A macro shot of a single one of the purple flower with a soft-focus background showing hints of bare soil tones." /></p>

<p>But it’s not always about trying to hide the ugly. I love to take a photo of a pretty fallen leaf on the sidewalk from down at the ground so that in the bokeh you can make out an old rusted out truck and a bunch of power lines.</p>

<p>I like to capture “life finds a way” subject. A rose snaking out from between fence posts, or a crack in the sidewalk with a tiny flower.</p>

</blockquote>

<h5>Some Additional Ideas and Tips from Bart</h5>

<p>I’m not an urban person, I grew up in rural Ireland, and I feel most a home surrounded by nature, so I struggle with urban photography, though I am starting to get better at it. </p>

<p>Here are some things that work for me:</p>

<ul>
<li><p>Keep an eye out at the smallest scales — lots of interesting shots hide in the small details!</p>

<ul>
<li>Like Allison, I’m fascinated by how nature finds a way, and happens most at small scales in thinks like walls and pavements</li>
<li>Proud citizens decorate urban public spaces at all scales, not just big monuments!</li>
<li>Proud residents decorate their homes in fun ways, especially at Halloween and over the winter holiday weeks.</li>

</ul>
</li>
<li><p>You can also get interesting results shooting a big scene wide but with something of interest very close to show a place at multiple scales at once</p>

</li>
<li><p>When shooting buildings, try really hard to keep the camera face-on and perfectly horizontal and level to minimise perspective distortions, especially when shooting wide</p>

<ul>
<li>If you can’t get your parallels perfectly parallel in-camera, spend some time with those three perspective sliders in post!</li>

</ul>
</li>
<li><p>Or … do the exactly opposite, and intentionally shoot wide and off-square to amplify the keystone effect!</p>

</li>
<li><p>Experiment with unusual points of view</p>

<ul>
<li>The iPhone’s superpower is how easy it is to shoot low, the world looks really different from down there!</li>
<li>I LOVE combining shooting small things with getting really close, wide, and low</li>

</ul>
</li>
<li><p>Contrasts can make for really cool shots</p>

<ul>
<li>Big &amp; small</li>
<li>Clashing colours</li>
<li>Textures, e.g. rough &amp; smooth</li>

</ul>
</li>
<li><p>Sometimes the cacophony of colours can distract more than it helps, so remember you always have the option to convert to monochrome!</p>

<ul>
<li>It’s no coincidence a lot of street photography is black &amp; white!</li>

</ul>
</li>

</ul>
<p>I also see others do greet things in urban settings that I’m trying to get a handle on, but so far, without much success:</p>

<ul>
<li><p>People! Were a fascinating bunch us humans, and there tend to be a lot of us in urban areas, but I’m just bad at photographing us, you might have more success </p>

</li>
<li><p>Look out for interesting shapes</p>

<ul>
<li>Takes an ability to mentally subtract most of the scene, detail, and colour that I’ve just not mastered</li>
<li>Colour often distracts here, so I see lots of stunning monochromes that make great use of shapes.</li>

</ul>
</li>

</ul>
