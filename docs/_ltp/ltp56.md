<h5>Ideas</h5>

<ul>
<li>Inspiration is an inverse bell curve — things that we approve of or like a lot can inspire us very strongly, things we don&#8217;t care about don&#8217;t inspire us, and things we strongly disapprove of or dislike a lot can inspire is very strongly too.</li>
<li>Everyone is comfortable with the positive side of the inverse bell curve, but some find the negative half somehow disconcerting, or worrying, or distasteful</li>
<li>To me, inspiration is one of the most positive forces in the universe because it allows us to turn something terrible into something good.</li>
<li>What inspires me to photograph?

<ul>
<li>Mostly my love of learning, and of sharing knowledge (trains, local landscapes, local history, nature &#8230;)</li>
<li>Sometimes, an emotional journey (monochrome historic cemeteries project)</li>
</ul></li>
<li>Does it make a difference?

<ul>
<li>Pulitzer prizes are very often than photos of human suffering caused by other humans — if photos of these things make a difference, why do they still happen?</li>
<li>Making a difference is not all or nothing — award winning journalistic photography does not end all human suffering, but that doesn&#8217;t mean it does nothing!</li>
<li>Prize-winning photos are by definition a selection, so while they do show that there is still suffering in 2018, they don&#8217;t tell us anything about how wide-spread that suffering is, or whether or not things are worse on average or better on average in 2018 than they were in 1918.</li>
<li>IMO, if a photo makes one person stop and think, it was worth while.</li>
<li>Change is very slow, and very incremental, but it&#8217;s built upon billions of little moments where someone was somehow inspired to think, to ask questions, or even just to feel.</li>
</ul></li>
</ul>
