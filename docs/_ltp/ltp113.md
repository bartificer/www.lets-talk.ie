# Classifiers

<ul>
<li>Pros

<ul>
<li>Easier searching of images without metadata</li>
<li>Accessibility for the visually impaired</li>
</ul></li>
<li>Cons

<ul>
<li>Atypical views of things are most likely not to get matched, and hence, get lost — biases in favour of samey shots and against more creative shots</li>
<li>Over confidence in the algorithms could discourage manual tagging, descriptions, etc, reducing the quality of metadata overall</li>
</ul></li>
</ul>

# ML Editors

<ul>
<li>Pros

<ul>
<li>Spectacular improvements in image quality from small sensors, i.e. why phone cameras don&#8217;t suck anymore!</li>
<li>Democratisation of image editing — regular folks get better images!</li>
<li>All editors of all levels get freed from drudgery with powerful automations like content aware fill</li>
</ul></li>
<li>Cons

<ul>
<li>The algorithms can end up defining the look of millions of people&#8217;s photos — the average rises, but the peaks fall too</li>
<li>It can be very hard to correct images when computational photography gets it wrong, much ML is now so engrained in the processes that it can&#8217;t be turned off!</li>
</ul></li>
</ul>

# Generative AI

<ul>
<li>Pros

<ul>
<li>Lets imaginative people who are not skilled image creators create amazing visual art</li>
<li>Can stimulate creativity</li>
<li>Democratises basic illustrations for articles, presentations, etc.</li>
<li>Allows the creation of otherwise impossible things</li>
</ul></li>
<li>Cons

<ul>
<li>Could put photographers out of a job</li>
<li>Could crowd out human creativity</li>
<li>The legal footing is simple in limbo — using generated images opens you up to real legal jeopardy because absolutely no one has any idea how the courts are going to treat this!</li>
</ul></li>
</ul>
