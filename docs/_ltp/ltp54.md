<h5>Outline Shownotes</h5>

<ul>
<li>The big-picture

<ul>
<li>Three concerns

<ul>
<li>Is it legal? (easiest to define and least subjective)</li>
<li>Is it moral? I.e., does it do less harm than good? (much more of a value judgement)</li>
<li>Do you just not like it?</li>
</ul></li>
<li>Things can be illegal but ethical, or legal but unethical

<ul>
<li>Copyright and patent laws doesn&#8217;t seem particularly moral these days</li>
<li>A lot of data collection is legal, but seems exploitative and hence unethical to me (US law is far behind EU law on this front)</li>
</ul></li>
<li>IMO It&#8217;s OK to express your dislikes, but it&#8217;s not OK to get up on a moral high horse about something you can&#8217;t rationally argue is immoral

<ul>
<li>Anything that breaks <a href="http://www.wheatonslaw.com">Wheaton&#8217;s Law</a> makes me cranky</li>
<li>Any time someone with a fancy-looking camera behaves in an anti-social way they give all photographers a bad name (it&#8217;s like the bad cyclists who break lights give all cyclists a bad name)</li>
<li>Just because you have a right to do something doesn&#8217;t mean you have a right to do it without being criticised! I have a right to call you out for being a dick!</li>
</ul></li>
</ul></li>
<li>The moral questions that matter most to me when I&#8217;m figuring out how I feel about something:

<ul>
<li>Is there deception going on?</li>
<li>Is there exploitation going on?</li>
</ul></li>
<li>Is <em>photoshopping</em> OK? (Triggered by the <a href="https://petapixel.com/2018/02/06/peter-lik-called-photographers-faked-moon-photo/">recent controversy over the fine art photo <em>Moonlit Dreams</em> by Peter Lik</a>)

<ul>
<li>IMO, photography is an art first, and a flat recording method second, so the default assumption should not be <em>this is straight out of the camera</em>.</li>
<li>Journalistic photography is the exception, not the rule. What makes it exceptional is the expectation that it&#8217;s a straight recording of reality.</li>
<li>IMO there is even room for artistic expression within documentary photography. There are many ways of telling a story, and straight emotionless purely factual images are just one of those.

<ul>
<li>Surely the great <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._Winston_Link">O. Winston Link</a> was documenting the dying days of steam railways? But his photos are anything but straight and emotionless!</li>
</ul></li>
<li>Unless Peter Lik was misrepresenting the shot as being anything but a work of art then I have zero problem with it being a composite.</li>
<li>IMO the real questions are things like <em>does it capture the majesty of our universe?</em>, <em>is it beautiful?</em> and <em>is it well execute?</em></li>
</ul></li>
</ul>
