<h5>Legal Latest</h5>

<ul>
<li>Apple and Nokia reach an agreement to end their patent dispute in which Apple paid Nokia and undisclosed sum of money and have agreed to on-going licensing payments, and Nokia is &#8220;providing certain network infrastructure product and services to Apple&#8221; - <a href="https://www.macobserver.com/news/apple-nokia-patent-licensing-settlement/">www.macobserver.com/&#8230;</a></li>
<li>In a case taken by food giant <em>Kraft</em> against beverage flavouring company <em>TC Heartland</em> the US Supreme Court have tightened the rules on patent law suits a little, making jurisdiction-shopping a little harder. The patent venue statute, 28 U. S. C. §1400(b), provides that <em>&#8220;[a]ny civil action for patent infringement may be brought in the judicial district where the defendant resides, or where the defendant has committed acts of infringement and has a regular and established place of business&#8221;</em> - the case revolved around the definition of <em>resides</em>, and the court found that <em>&#8220;As applied to domestic corporations, &#8216;reside[nce]&#8217; in §1400(b) refers only to the State of incorporation&#8221;</em> - <a href="http://www.reuters.com/article/us-usa-court-kraft-heinz-idUSKBN18I1SZ">www.reuters.com/&#8230;</a></li>
<li>Security company RSA has sued Apple &amp; Visa, claiming Apple Pay infringes their patents - <a href="https://www.macobserver.com/news/rsa-apple-pay-patent-infringement/">www.macobserver.com/&#8230;</a></li>
</ul>
