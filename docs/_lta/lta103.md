<h4>Main Stories</h4>

<ol>
<li>&#x1f1ea;&#x1f1fa; The EU Commission, EU Parliament &amp; Council of Ministers strike a deal on the <em>Digital Markets Act</em> (DMA), an EU directive aimed at tackling anti-trust concerns around big tech.

<ul>
<li>The law will only apply to <strong>gatekeepers</strong> that provide <strong>core platform services</strong> (browsers, OSes, market places, app stores, messaging services, etc.)</li>
<li>To be designated a gatekeeper a company must both:</li>
<li>Have a market cap of €75Bn or annual revenue of €7.5Bn</li>
<li>Have at least 45M active weekly users in the EU</li>
<li>Have at least 10K active business customers in the EU</li>
<li>Gatekeepers will have extra responsibilities, and there will be explicit prohibitions</li>
<li>While there is agreement between the three parties needed to pass the act, the technical wording has not been finalised.</li>
<li>The most significant impacts on Apple appear to be:

<ul>
<li>Additional choices on iOS/macOS since gatekeepers must <em>&#8220;allow users to freely choose their browser, virtual assistants or search engines&#8221;</em>.</li>
<li>Apple may be forced to provide APIs to allow smaller messaging services interoperate with Messages. EU officials have said they want end-to-end encryption to be a part of these integrations.</li>
<li>It seems clear Apple will be forced to allow alternative payment mechanisms, and probably some kind of alternative to the app store, but probably not wild-west side-loading, with Commission staff talking about <em>&#8220;multiple safe and secure app stores&#8221;</em> rather than full-on side-loading.</li>
<li>Apple may also have to share more AppStore sales data with developers.</li>
<li>The ban on <em>&#8216;self preferencing&#8217;</em> will probably have some effect on how Apple treats its own apps in the AppStore.</li>
<li>Apple will probably be forced to open up access to things like the NFC chips in iPhones &amp; iPads since gatekeepers are required to give developers <em>‘fair’</em> access to <em>‘supplementary functionalities’</em> on mobile devices. (I wonder if that could extend to the W1 radio too?)</li>
</ul></li>
