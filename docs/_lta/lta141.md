<li><p>The Assault on the App Store ramps up on both sides of the Atlantic</p>

<ul>
<li><p> The fallout from the anti-steering injunction in the <strong>Apple -v- Epic</strong> case continued through May</p>

<ul>
<li><p>Where things stood last month:</p>

<ul>
<li><p>Epic lost almost all of its case against Apple</p>

</li>
<li><p>Apple lost on one point out of 11 — their anti-steering rules were anti-competitive, that is to say, the rule that blocked apps from not only linking to external payment options, but prevented apps from <strong>even mentioning</strong> their existence!</p>

</li>
<li><p>The court imposed an injunction on Apple forcing them to alter their rules on anti-steering to come into compliance with the ruling, even while the appeal slowly worked its way through the process.</p>

</li>
<li><p>Apple responded to the injunction with a new App Store Entitlement for the US only that allowed external links, but with conditions:</p>

<ul>
<li>An arguably OTT warning dialog telling users they are leaving the app and going to the open web</li>
<li>One single link destination for every single out-bound link, no linking directly to specific products!</li>
<li>a 27% fee not just on all purchases made directly, but also all purchases made over the next 5 days</li>
<li>On-demand access to audited accounts so Apple could verify their cut</li>

</ul>
</li>
<li><p>Epic filed an appeal against Apple&#39;s change saying it was so clearly designed to be so utterly unappealing that in effect, Apple did not remove their ban (a rare case where I agree with Epic!</p>

</li>
<li><p>In an utterly scathing ruling at the end of last month (April 2025) the judge agreed with Epic, and ordered Apple to just allow direct link outs, full stop, end of story.</p>

</li>
<li><p>Apple said they would appeal</p>

</li>
<li><p>Even though the injunction was purely about link outs, and not about all the other points in the case, Epic publicly promised to return Fortnight to the App Store in the US soon</p>

</li>
<li><p>The kinds of apps most affected by the onerous rules like Patreon released updates within days adding buy buttons to their US apps.</p>

</li>
<li><p><strong>Related:</strong> A good presentation of the argument that the whole anti-steering thing was always nuts, and that Apple should have gotten off this train years ago — <a href='https://daringfireball.net/linked/2025/05/06/amazon-kindle-get-book'>daringfireball.net/…</a> (I agree with this, it&#39;s not about whether have the <strong>right</strong> to implement a dumb user-hostile rule, but whether they <strong>should</strong>)</p>

</li>

<ul>
<li><p>Summary of where stings stood at the end of April:</p>

<li><p>May started with Apple taking over the narrative with a strong appeal — <a href='https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/05/08/apple-appeals-570-million-antitrust-fine-says-eu-blew-off-compliance-collaboration'>appleinsider.com/…</a></p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Context:</strong> EU laws are initially drafted by the commission, but they then have to enter into negotiation with the parliament and the council of ministers to agree a final wording that can pass a vote in both of those bodies to become law. This process generally results in reasonable compromises between business and consumer interests.</li>
<li>The DMA took years to get approved, and there were a lot of tweaks and changes, resulting the commission not getting things its own way on everything, including the addition of wording that the commission should engage constructively with companies they deem to be breaching the law before resorting to fines.</li>
<li>Apple&#39;s appeal bluntly states that under now departed commissioner Margrethe Vestager, their attempts to work with the commission were ignored and even rebuffed, giving an example of an offer to completely remove the anti-steering provisions which they claim they were told to hold off on until the commission had time to hear from all parties.</li>
<li>The focus of the EU&#39;s finding against Apple is that very anti-steering rule Apple provided evidence of offering to remove.</li>
<li><strong>Analysis &amp; Opinion:</strong> <a href='https://daringfireball.net/linked/2025/05/15/apple-ec-proposals-feedback'>Apple, Appealing €500M DMA Fine, Contends It Made a Series of Proposals to the European Commission Throughout 2024 but Did Not Receive Feedback — daringfireball.net/…</a></li>
<li><strong>Opinion by Bart:</strong> my reading of all this is that Apple have a point here, Vestager really did appear to be openly hostile to Apple, and I think she wanted their scalp as a trophy to help her build her post-commission career.</li>

</ul>
