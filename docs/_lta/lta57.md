<h4>Main Stories</h4>

<ol>
<li>An eventful month for Apple&#8217;s App Stores

<ul>
<li>Probably related to GDPR, Apple has started to crack down on apps that share location data with third parties — <a href="https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2018/05/apple-reportedly-removing-apps-that-share-location-data-with-third-parties/">arstechnica.com/…</a>

<ul>
<li>Apps that are not asking for clear enough consent before sharing location data with a third party are being pulled from the store, with developers told to remove the sharing or ask for clear consent and then re-submit the apps.</li>
<li><p>Apple has also tightened the rules on what third parties can do with the location data after its shared with them:</p>

<blockquote>
<p>Data collected from apps may not be used or shared with third parties for purposes unrelated to improving the user experience or software/hardware performance connected to the app’s functionality, or to serve advertising in compliance with the Apple Developer Program License Agreement</p>
</blockquote></li>
</ul></li>
<li>Government Censorship

<ul>
<li>Apple was forced to remove apps that use CallKit (facilitates VOIP calling) from their Chinese app store — <a href="https://www.imore.com/apple-removing-callkit-apps-china-because-china">www.imore.com/…</a> &amp; <a href="https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2018/05/apple-cracks-down-on-callkit-enabled-apps-in-chinas-app-store/">arstechnica.com/…</a></li>
<li>Russia forced Apple to remove Telegram from their Russian stores, and there seems to have been some collateral damage, with the Telegram developers reporting that they can&#8217;t submit app updates for their apps at all ATM — <a href="https://www.imore.com/russia-asks-apple-remove-messaging-app-telegram-app-store">www.imore.com/…</a> &amp; <a href="https://arstechnica.com/?p=1316769">arstechnica.com/…</a></li>
<li>Starting with the next reporting period (from July 2018), Apple will include government app removal requests in their regular transparency reports — <a href="https://www.macobserver.com/columns-opinions/editorial/apple-to-begin-reporting-government-app-store-removal-requests/">www.macobserver.com/…</a></li>
</ul></li>
<li><p>A small number of indie developers have banded together to form <em>The Developers Union</em>, and ask Apple to bring free trials to the store by WWDC 2019, they plan to move on to campaign for other app store changes that will benefit developers — <a href="https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2018/05/ios-devs-form-a-non-union-union-ask-apple-to-allow-free-trials-on-app-store/">arstechnica.com/…</a>, <a href="https://www.macobserver.com/news/indie-developers-union-apple/">www.macobserver.com/…</a> &amp; <a href="https://www.imore.com/developers-union-responds-apples-policies-open-letter">www.imore.com/…</a></p></li>
<li>The Steam Link Controversy

<ul>
<li>Apple initially approved a Steam Link app for iOS — the app is basically screen sharing for gaming, allowing an iOS user to remotely play a game served from a steam-connected device on the same local area network — <a href="https://www.imore.com/valve-steam-link-app-will-soon-let-you-play-steam-games-android-and-ios">www.imore.com/…</a> &amp; <a href="https://www.imore.com/steam-link-and-steam-video-apps-are-coming-iphone-ipad-and-apple-tv">www.imore.com/…</a></li>
<li>After Valve publicly announced that Steam Link had been approved, Apple changed their mind, and yanked the app — <a href="https://arstechnica.com/?p=1314443">arstechnica.com/…</a></li>
</ul></li>
<li>Microsoft put pressure on Apple by lowering their cut on their store (for most, but not all, purchases) — <a href="https://blogs.windows.com/buildingapps/2018/05/07/a-new-microsoft-store-revenue-share-is-coming/">blogs.windows.com/…</a>

<ul>
<li>Games remain at a 70/30% split</li>
<li>Consumer apps get an 85/15% split when Microsoft actively sends the customer to the developer through some kind of promotion</li>
<li>Otherwise, consumers app get a 95/5% split</li>
</ul></li>
</ul></li>

</ul></li>
