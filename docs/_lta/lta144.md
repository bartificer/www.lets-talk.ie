<li><p> The UK&#39;s super-secret case against Apple got significantly more confusing in August:</p>

<ul>
<li><strong>Reminder:</strong> under the UK&#39;s so-called <em>snooper&#39;s charter</em> the government can secretly order technological changes and it is a crime for the recipients of the recipients of such orders to admit they received them. The only think we know for absolute certain is that Apple removed it&#39;s <em>Advanced Data Protection</em> feature from UK users because it as not longer able to offer it.</li>
<li>The last we had heard about the story was reporting from the FT in London that the UK government was trying to find a face-saving way to back out of the whole thing.</li>
<li> The first development in late August was a Tweet from the US Director of National Intelligence Tulsi Gabbard saying UK had dropped their attempt to get at the data of US residents, but there was no official confirmation from the UK government, or from Apple, and ADP remains unavailable in the UK — <a href='https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/08/19/uk-blinks-backs-down-from-its-apple-backdoor-encryption-demand'>appleinsider.com/…</a></li>
<li>The media initially took this one short and vague Tweet as proof the whole things was over, but just a week later the FT returned with another bombshell of a story — they have seen Apple&#39;s submissions for a secret court hearing on the whole thing and it is set for <em>early 2026</em>. Not just that, but the British governments demands are reported to go far beyond just a back door in ADP — <a href='https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/08/29/uk-icloud-data-demand-wanted-far-more-than-the-worlds-messages'>appleinsider.com/…</a></li>
<li>So, we end the month like we started it, none the wiser as to how all this will play out </li>
