<li>The security provided by Apple&#8217;s new T2 chips comes with some tradeoffs to repairability

<ul>
<li><a href="https://www.theverge.com/2018/11/12/18077166/apple-macbook-air-mac-mini-t2-chip-security-repair-replacement-tool">Apple confirms its T2 security chip blocks some third-party repairs of new Macs — www.theverge.com/…</a>
 &gt; The parts affected […] are the <strong>display assembly</strong>, <strong>logic board</strong>, <strong>top case</strong>, and <strong>Touch ID board</strong> for the MacBook Pro, and the logic board and <strong>flash storage</strong> on the iMac Pro. It is also likely that logic board repairs on the new MacBook Air and Mac mini are affected, as well as the Mac mini’s flash storage</li>
<li><a href="https://www.imore.com/regarding-t2-security-chips-blocking-independent-mac-repairs">Regarding T2 security chips &#8216;blocking&#8217; independent Mac repairs&#8230; — www.imore.com/…</a></li>
</ul></li>
<li>The US Supreme Court heard oral arguments in a case testing whether or not App Store customers have standing to sue Apple over allegations that its app store is anti-competitive.

<ul>
<li>In the lower courts, Apple succeeded in arguing that the class action suit should not be permitted to continue.</li>
<li>Apple&#8217;s arguments include the fact that it does not set app prices, developers do, and developers are free to set low or high prices, right down to free.</li>
<li>Apple used a 1977 supreme court decision (<em>Illinois Brick Co. v. Illinois</em>) which found that only consumers who are direct purchasers of a product can bring an anti-trust lawsuit, and when it. comes to the App store that&#8217;s the developers, not iOS and Mac users, they are buying apps from developers, not from Apple.</li>
<li>The questions the justices asked imply they are leaning towards overturning the lower court&#8217;s decision — <a href="http://amylhowe.com/2018/11/26/argument-analysis-justices-poised-to-allow-antitrust-dispute-against-apple-over-apps-to-go-forward/">amylhowe.com/…</a></li>
<li>Should Apple lose the case, the result would be that an anti-trust trial could start.</li>
<li>The court is expected to rule next year</li>
</ul></li>
