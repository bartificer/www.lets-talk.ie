<h5 id="toc_1">Followups</h5>

<ul>
<li>Apple say that the extra battery monitoring they added to iOS 10.2 late last year has born fruit. It highlighted some problems which were addressed in iOS 10.2.1 which was released recently, and on-going monitoring has shown a marked reduction in unexpected shutdowns on iPhones 6 and 6S - <a href="http://www.imore.com/apple-and-ios-1021-address-unexpected-shutdowns-iphone-6-iphone-6s">www.imore.com/...</a> &amp; <a href="https://arstechnica.com/apple/2017/02/apple-says-ios-10-2-1-mostly-fixes-unexpected-shutdowns-in-the-iphone-6-6s/">arstechnica.com/...</a></li>
<li>Apple ratchets up it&#39;s fight with Australian banks over ApplePay, calling the large banks a <em>cartel</em> (the banks want to negotiate as one block, they asked permission from the regulator and they said no &amp; Apple has always been against the idea) - <a href="https://www.macobserver.com/news/australia-apple-pay-standoff/">www.macobserver.com/...</a> &amp; <a href="http://www.imore.com/apple-pay-chief-warns-australian-banks-get-behind-mobile-payments-or-risk-losing-customers">www.imore.com/...</a></li>
</ul>

<h5 id="toc_2">Notable Numbers</h5>

<ul>
<li>Gartner release their worldwide smartphone <strong>sales</strong> numbers for Q4 of 2016, and 2016 overall - <a href="http://www.gartner.com/newsroom/id/3609817">www.gartner.com/...</a>

<ul>
<li>Vendor breakdown for all of 2016 shows Samsung at top with 20.5% of sales, followed by Apple with 14.4%, and Huawei with 8.9%</li>
<li>Vendor breakdown for 2016 Q4 tells a different story though - Apple JUST edges out Samsung for the top stop with 17.9% agains Samsung&#39;s 17.8%. Again, Huawei gets the third spot with 9.5%</li>
<li>The OS breakdown for 2016 Q4 shows Blackberry OS at 0.0%, and Windows Phone at 0.3%. That 0.3% is enough to net Microsoft third place behind iOS at 17.9%, and Android at 81.7%</li>
</ul></li>
</ul>
