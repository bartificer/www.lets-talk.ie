<ul>
<li><p> The Digital Markets Act (DMA) continues to drive Apple news:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Beta versions of iOS 17.5 include support for website-based app distribution in the EU, so we can now see how it works, developers are cranky at the amount of warnings screens users see, and one security researcher warns that it may inadvertently expose EU iOS users to cross-site tracking — <a href='https://appleinsider.com/articles/24/04/16/how-ios-web-distribution-works-in-the-eu-in-ios-175'>appleinsider.com/…</a>, <a href='https://www.macobserver.com/news/apple-criticized-for-messy-eu-alternative-app-store-implementation/'>www.macobserver.com/…</a> &amp; <a href='https://www.mysk.blog/2024/04/28/safari-tracking/'>www.mysk.blog/…</a></p>
</li>
<li><p>The European Commission has ruled that because it is depended on by enough business users, iPadOS does fall under the DMA after all, giving Apple 6 months to comply, which Apple have confirmed they will do — <a href='https://www.macobserver.com/ipados/eu-deems-ipados-a-gatekeeper-despite-not-meeting-user-threshold/'>www.macobserver.com/…</a> &amp; <a href='https://www.macstories.net/news/apple-changes-how-the-core-technology-fee-works-and-confirms-that-its-alternative-business-terms-will-apply-to-ipad-apps-this-fall/'>www.macstories.net/…</a></p>
</li>
<li><p>Apple announced their promised changes to the controversial <em>Core Technology Fee</em> (CTF) that applies to developers who opt for Apple&#39;s EU-only iOS app store terms — <a href='https://www.cultofmac.com/855180/apple-exempts-freeware-from-controversial-core-technology-fee/'>www.cultofmac.com/…</a></p>
<ul>
<li>Zero-profit developers like hobbyists and charities are now completely exempted — it&#39;s not about the app, it&#39;s about the developer, and if you make money by putting ads in your apps you are definitely not zero-profit (this addresses the <em>&#39;my kid made a great app that went viral and now I own Apple a small fortune&#39;</em> problem)</li>
<li>Small developers (&lt;€10M annual revenue) get an on-ramp to the CTF via a three-year exemption, and if they poke over the €10M mark within those three years, the CTF is called at €1M</li>

</ul>
</li>
<li><p>Reporting by Reuters and others suggests the iOS browser choice screen mandated by the DMA may be having an effect, users seem to be choosing privacy-focused browsers, and Opera is reporting 63% growth — <a href='https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2024/04/report-people-are-bailing-on-safari-after-dma-makes-changing-defaults-easier/'>arstechnica.com/…</a> &amp; <a href='https://www.macobserver.com/ios/opera-on-ios-sets-an-example-of-how-much-has-dma-affected-the-competition-reports-63-growth/'>www.macobserver.com/…</a></p>
