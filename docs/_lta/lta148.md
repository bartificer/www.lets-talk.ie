<li><p>&#x1f1fa;&#x1f1f8; <strong>Apple -v- Epic:</strong> an three-judge panel on the US Ninth Circuit Court of Appeals has upheld up most, but not all, of the injunction preventing Apple from charging <strong>any</strong> commission on external in-app payments in the US app store — <a href='https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/12/11/appeals-court-amends-order-to-let-apple-resume-third-party-commissions'>appleinsider.com/…</a> &amp; <a href='https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2025/12/epic-celebrates-the-end-of-the-apple-tax-after-appeals-court-win-in-ios-payments-case/'>arstechnica.com/…</a> (this was Apple&#39;s appeal)</p>

<ul>
<li><p>The ruling is nuanced, but on the whole, seems like a loss for Apple:</p>

<ul>
<li>The judges agreed with Judge Gonzalez Rogers that Apple violated the anti-steering injunction she placed on them by setting the commission on external in-app payments at 27% (in response to which she blocked all commissions)</li>
<li>But the judges disagreed with the remedy (removing all commissions), say Apple can charge <strong>something</strong>, but it needs to be reasonable.</li>
<li>Rather than deciding on what would be reasonable, the panel ordered that Apple  propose a new fee structure which Epic must then agree to before the collection of commissions can resume.</li>

</ul>
</li>
<li><p>Predictably, Epic thinks anything more than zero is too much — <a href='https://appleinsider.com/articles/25/12/12/epic-games-signals-it-wont-agree-to-percentage-app-store-fees'>appleinsider.com/…</a></p>
